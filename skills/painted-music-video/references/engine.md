# Engine reference

The engine is ClaudeAnimationBase (MIT, see `LICENSE-ClaudeAnimationBase`), extended for music videos. p5.js and
p5.brush paint each frame in a WebGL canvas, and headless Chrome (puppeteer-core) renders frames in parallel.
ffmpeg encodes them with the song.

## 2. Frames are pure functions of time

- **Frames render in parallel and out of order.** A shot is `fn(t, lt, dur)`, and it must draw the same frame for the
  same `t` every time. Don't carry state between frames: no counters that survive, no `Math.random()`, no physics
  integrated frame by frame. Compute everything from `t` in closed form.
- **Randomness:** use `hash(i)` for stable per-object values. `jit(a)`/`random()` are seeded, and with `BOILN = 0`
  they're the same every frame.
- **Seed each element with `boilSeed(key)`,** a key that is the same every frame and unique within it. Do it before
  each background layer, prop and effect. `paint()`/`inkLine()` then reseed per shape, so one shape's texture never
  depends on the shapes drawn before it.
- **Characters take a `boilKey`.** Without one, their key is their call order in the frame (`PERSON_N`, `CLAWD_N`),
  which changes when characters come and go mid-shot. A new character type with its own counter must be reset per
  frame: `FRAME_RESETS.push(() => { MASCOT_N = 0; })`.
- **Each shot paints the whole frame,** background included. The paper is under everything and the grain is
  multiplied over the top.
- **Canvas:** 1920×1080, origin top-left, y down.

## 3. Painting

`paint(pts, o)` paints one shape from `[[x, y], ...]`:

| option | meaning |
|---|---|
| `wash, washOp` | flat colour (opacity 0–255, default 255): characters, props, anything solid |
| `fill, fillOp, bleed, tex, border` | watercolour fill with bleeding edges and pigment texture: skies, hills, shading, shadows. `bleed` ~.05–.3, `tex` ~.3–.9 |
| `hatch: { d, a, o, b, c, w }` | hatching (distance, angle, `{rand, gradient}`, brush, colour, weight), sparingly |
| `ink, sw, br` | outline colour (default `PAL.ink`), weight (~.4–2), brush. **`ink: null` = no outline** |
| `curv` | 0–1: smooth the outline through the points |

- **Lines:** `inkLine(pts, sw, colour, brush = 'ink', curvature)`. The brushes are `'ink'`, `'inkfine'` and
  `'dry'`, plus p5.brush's `'2B'`, `'HB'`, `'charcoal'`, `'marker'`, `'pen'`, `'cpencil'` and `'spray'`.
- **Shapes:**
  - `rectPts(x, y, w, h, j)`, `ellPts(cx, cy, rx, ry, n, j, rot)` and `rrPts(x, y, w, h, r, j)` (rounded)
  - `starPts(cx, cy, r, inner, n, rot)` and `heartPts(cx, cy, r)`
  - `through(P)`: a smooth curve through the points
  - `ribbon(P, w0, w1)`: a tapered outline along a path, for arms, tails, vines, swooshes and painted glyphs
- **One shape, one outline.** Build things from as few outlines as possible, so they don't look like stickers.
- **Light:** `glow(x, y, r, colour, a)` adds light. Draw it before whatever sits in front of the light.
- **Palette:** `PAL`, plus `mixCol(a, b, k)`. Add the project's colours to `PAL` in `core.js`.

## 4. Time, beats and lyrics

- **Progress:**
  - `seg(t, a, b)`: 0..1 through [a, b], the building block for one-shot actions
  - `kf(t, [[t0, v0], ...], ease)`: keyframes, with numbers or arrays as values
  - `lerp`, `clamp`, `frac` and `TAU`
- **Easing:** `ease`, `easeIn`, `easeOut`, `backOut` (overshoot) and `elasticOut`.
- **Beats** (from `BEATS`, the real track):
  - `bpOf(t)`: the beat position
  - `beatN(t)`: the beat number
  - `beatT(b)`: the time of beat b; `beatT(4 * bar)` is a downbeat
  - `pulse(t, k)`: 1 on each beat, then decays; `pulse2` does the same on eighths
  - Use these for hits, cuts, light flashes and props, not for idle body motion.
- **Lyrics** (from `LY`):
  - `lineT(i)` and `lineEnd(i)`: line i's start and end
  - `wordT(i, k)`: the start of line i's k-th word
  - `wordAt(i, 'free')`: the first word in line i containing 'free'
  - Key shots to these, not to typed seconds.
- **Acting:**
  - `jump(t, t0, t1, h)` → `{dy, sq}` and `take(t, t0, amt)` → `{sq, dy}`
  - `stroll(t, t0, t1, x0, x1, u)` → `{x, walk, view, flip, dy}`
  - `spring(t, t0, k, w)` and `ring(t, [t0, ...])`: damped wobbles for settles
  - `arcPt(p0, p1, h, k)`: a point on a throw
  - `onTwos(t)`: hold each drawing for two frames, if you want that look

## 5. Camera and full-frame effects

- **Camera:** `camBegin(cx, cy, zoom, rot)` … `camEnd()` puts world point (cx, cy) at the screen centre. It's one
  level only, so always pair the two calls. `toScreen(x, y)` maps world to screen while a camera is active (to aim
  an iris at a face).
- **Effects** (screen space, after `camEnd()`):
  - `brushWipe(p, [c1, c2])`: covers the frame from p 0 to .5, then uncovers it to 1. Cut under full cover; the
    two-call pattern is in its comment.
  - `iris(cx, cy, r, colour)` and `irisShape(pts, colour)`: shaped reveals
  - `flash(k, colour)`: a full-frame flash
- `shakeXY()` exists for compatibility and always returns `[0, 0]`.

## 6. Characters: person(), clawd(), emotions, new characters

### person(x, y, s, o): a chibi human

(x, y) is the ground point between the feet. s is the unit: the figure is about 13.5s tall and its head 5s wide.
In body space (s units) the feet are at y 0, the hips at −3.3, the shoulders at (±1.9, −7.2), the head centre at
(0, −10.3), the eyes at (±.92, −10.05) and the mouth at (0, −8.95).

| group | options |
|---|---|
| look | `look` (a key of `LOOKS`), `style` (override any look field), `tint`/`tintK`, `blush`, `gloom`, `bags` (eye bags 0..1), `frizz` (hair standing up 0..1) |
| pose | `dx`, `dy` (in s), `sq`, `rot`, `flip`, `walk` (leg phase), `run`, `sit`, `back` (seen from behind), `kick` (±1, a side kick), `noShadow`, `noLegs` |
| arms | `aLp`/`aRp`: shoulder angle (0 = straight out, + = up, about −1.3 = hanging). `bendL`/`bendR`: elbow bend (+ lifts the forearm). Or `aL`/`aR` in Clawd's convention (from emotions). **`gripL`/`gripR: [x, y]`** in WORLD space solves the arm so the hand lands there (natural elbow; `elbowL`/`elbowR` true/false forces a side; `gripLk` blends). `reachL`/`reachR` do the same in body units. |
| hands | `holdL`/`holdR(s, sw)` draw upright at the hand (a mug, a phone); `handL`/`handR(s, sw)` draw along the forearm; `draw(s, sw)` paints in body space, on top |
| face | `eyes` (Clawd's eye names), `mouth` (Clawd's mouths, or `singMouth(t)`), `lookX`/`lookY`, `squint`, `brows` (`'worried' \| 'angry' \| 'up' \| 'flat'`), `emote`/`emoteK`/`emoteAge`, `seed` (blink timing) |
| seeds | `boilKey`: a stable id (set it whenever characters come and go) |

It returns `{ hL, hR, head, top, eye }` in world space, so props and other characters can meet the hands.
`feel()` and `emotions()` spread straight in; their arm angles are mapped to human arms.

**LOOKS** fields:
- colours: `skin`, `hair`, `top`/`topDk`, `shirt`, `pants`, `shoes`
- `hairStyle`: `mop`, `swoop`, `slick`, `bald`, `bun` or `short`
- `outfit`: `cardigan`, `hoodie`, `suit` (+ `tie`), `vest`, `trench` or `overalls`
- extras: `glasses` (`round` or `reading`), `mustache`, `lanyard`, `pen`

Replace the example looks with the song's cast. For anything the presets can't do (a uniform, a hat, long hair, a
skirt), add a branch in `person()` the same way the outfits are drawn.

### clawd(x, y, u, o): a blocky mascot, and the emotion system

Clawd is a terracotta block 10u wide and 8u tall, with stubby legs and arm nubs. Its options are the same kinds as
person's:
- `view` (`front`, `q`, `side`, `qback` or `back`, turning through `turn()`/`spinView()`), `smear`
- `aL`/`aR`, `armL`/`armR(u, sw)` hooks at the arm tips, `hat`, and `draw(u, sw)`
- `lid` (the top opens like a lunchbox, for fury or chomping)
- `tint`, or `col`/`dk`/`lt` for its body colours

Use it as a non-human character, or as the model for one.

- **`feel(name, t, over)`:** one emotion, as a face, a colour and a fixed resting pose (calm: no idle motion).
- **`emotions(t, [[t0, name, over?], ...])`:** acted changes. It squints and squashes just before each change, swaps
  the face, cross-fades the colour, fires a small take and pops the emote.
- **Emotions:**

  | family | emotions |
  |---|---|
  | joy | happy, excited, laugh, love, proud, starstruck, playful, hopeful, relieved |
  | sly | smug, cool, mischief, suspicious |
  | low | sad, cry, bored, sleepy, ko |
  | hot | angry, furious, determined, disgusted |
  | alarm | scared, nervous, surprised, confused, dizzy |
  | mind | neutral, thinking, idea, shy |

- **Eyes:** normal, look, wide, happy, closed, sleepy, wink, narrow, angry, determined, sad, teary, cry, squeeze,
  shine, scared, blank, spark, red, heart, x, swirl, dot, shades.
- **Mouths:** o, O, smile, grin, flat, wobble, cat, frown, smirk, laugh, open, wail, teeth, tongue, pout, yawn.
- **Emotes:** `!` `?` `!!` `!?` zzz, sweat, spark, heart, hearts, anger, steam, bulb, dots, scribble, music, swirl,
  stars, cloud. `emote(kind, x, y, s, k, age)` draws one anywhere.
- **`move(style, t, seed)`:** damped dances (bounce, hop, roof, sway, spin, wave, walk, run, idle, stomp, shimmy,
  mix).

### Making a new character (a mascot, an animal, a creature, a talking object)

Write `function name(x, y, s, o = {})` in its own file, following `person()`:
1. **Give it an id.** Use `o.boilKey` or a counter (`let NAME_N = 0; FRAME_RESETS.push(() => { NAME_N = 0; });`). Call
   `boilSeed('<name> ' + id + ' <part>')` before each part.
2. **Accept the emotion fields** so `feel()`/`emotions()` drive it: `eyes`, `mouth`, `lookX`/`lookY`, `squint`,
   `blush`, `emote`/`emoteK`/`emoteAge`, `sq`, `rot`, `dx`/`dy`, and `aL`/`aR` if it has arms. Reuse `eyes()`,
   `mouth()` and `emote()` from clawd.js where they fit.
3. **Return world positions** of its hands, head and top, for props and for the camera.
4. **Mind the colour cross-fade.** During a mood change, `emotions()` sets `o.col`/`o.dk`/`o.lt` from Clawd's
   terracotta tints. A character with its own body colour must ignore those (use its own `bodyCol`), or it flashes
   orange for half a second at every mood change.
5. **Light marks on a dark body** (eyes, highlights, patterns) must be full-opacity washes (or `ribbon` shapes painted
   as washes). Thin translucent strokes mix into the body colour and vanish.
6. **Add it to `LOOPS.cast`** (or its own loop), and check it at every emotion the storyboard uses.

## 7. Props

The starter kit in `props.js`:
- `desk(x0, y, w, h)` and `mug(x, y, s, {steam, tilt, col})`
- `lamp(x, y, s, {on, flip})`
- `bubbleRim(x, y, rx, ry, k, {tx, ty})`: a daydream bubble with its trail; draw the dream inside the ellipse
  first. `ellBelow` helps clip shapes to it.
- `pine(x, y, h, col)`, `canoe(x, y, s)` and `skyline(x0, y, w, {lights, snow, tower})`
- `curtains(t, open, {title})`, `titleCard(t, k)` (`PROJECT.title`), `theatreFrame(t)` and `spotCone(x, top, y, w, k)`
- `clockFace(x, y, r, hour)`
- `confetti(t, t0, n, cols, {leaves})`
- `phoneProp(x, y, s, {screen})`

Write the song's own props in the same pattern:
- one function per object, with (x, y) a stated anchor and s a scale;
- states as 0..1 numbers (`open`, `on`, `fill`);
- `boilSeed` first;
- painted with `paint`/`inkLine`.

## 8. Karaoke, lettering and the logo

- **Karaoke** runs automatically from `LY`: a painted band at y 975–1070, with each word sweeping to gold as it's
  sung.
  - `KARAOKE_STYLE = t => ({ band, fill, text, sung })` recolours it over the song (a palette arc).
  - `window.NO_KARAOKE = true` turns it off (in a loop, or on the end card).
- **Lettering:** `letter(txt, x, y, size, colour, {pop, rot, alpha, stroke, font, screen})` and
  `sfx(txt, x, y, size, colour, age)`. Both are composited at `flushLetters()`; call it yourself before a wipe that
  must cover them.
- **The logo:** set `PROJECT.logo: 'assets/logo.png'` and call `logoAt(x, y, w, h, alpha)` (screen pixels). It's
  composited over the grain, exactly as designed. Paint a plaque in the logo's own background colour behind it so the
  bitmap's edges vanish, and keep the bitmap's aspect ratio.
- **AFTER_SHOT(t):** a hook that runs after every shot in screen space, for something that lives across shots (a
  corner clock, a progress meter).

## 9. Loops (model sheets and reference stills)

`LOOPS.name = t => {...}; LOOPS.name.len = 4;` makes a standalone loop. Scrub it at `studio.html?loop=name`, or render
it with `--loop=name` added to any render command. The built-in loops:
- `emotions` and `views`: Clawd
- `cast`: every look, plus the lead in six emotions
- `arms`: reach targets; every hand should land on its dot
- `ref_<look>`: one character, full body, on plain paper, for thumbnails, posters or other tools' character uploads

## 10. Pitfalls

- **A NaN in a point list** makes p5.brush throw deep inside (`OffscreenCanvas ... unsigned long`). `centred()` skips
  such shapes and warns once with a stack. Guard geometry that can degenerate (`Math.acos` of a ratio > 1, division by
  a zero length).
- **Don't name a local `T`** (or `W`, `H`, `PAL`). `T` is the global frame time, used by blinks, steam and waves;
  shadowing it in a character function gives NaNs or frozen motion.
- **Units:** `dx`/`dy` in a pose are in character units (s or u), not pixels. A pixel offset passed as `dx` throws
  the character off frame.
- **Zoomed strokes:**
  - p5.brush loses strokes drawn far from the origin under a zoomed camera. `paint()`/`inkLine()` draw each shape
    around its own centre, which avoids it, but a shape much bigger than the canvas can still lose its outline.
  - Outline weight is in world units, so it grows with zoom. Scale `sw` down for small shapes in close-ups.
- **Pigment mixing:** a light colour over a dark one needs `washOp: 255` (or `glow()` for light). Yellow over blue
  turns green.
- **A degenerate watercolour fill** can throw. `paintAt` falls back to a flat wash and warns once.
- **Harmless warnings:** five `WebGL: INVALID_OPERATION` lines once per page, and a `willReadFrequently` note from the
  checks. Any other page error is real.
- **Cost** is the number of fill shapes and strokes: hundreds are fine, thousands are not. Aim for ≤ 1 s per frame;
  sheets print ms/frame.
- **GPU flags:** macOS uses `--use-angle=metal`; Linux without a GPU needs `--soft-gl` (slow); a Linux NVIDIA node
  takes `--gpu-angle=vulkan` (check with `node gpu_probe.mjs <chrome>`).
